Kolkrabbi
vs OpenCode

OpenCode is the closest thing to a sibling on this list: MIT, open source, terminal-first, model-agnostic, written in TypeScript, with 75+ providers through the AI SDK and Models.dev, configurable LSP and MCP servers, a desktop app and an IDE extension. It is also far larger — over 200,000 stars. Kolkrabbi reaches for the same ground from a different direction: one static Go binary, a dial that chooses models rather than thinking tokens, and a local record of what every model cost and how good you judged it.

Every claim below was checked against primary documentation on 2026-09-03. Both projects move quickly. If something here is out of date, it is a bug — say so.

01 / WHERE KOLKRABBI IS BEHIND

What OpenCode does better.

This section comes first on purpose. A comparison that opens with its own strengths is an advertisement, and you should not trust the rest of it.

Kolkrabbi does notLSP

No LSP

OpenCode configures language servers. Kolkrabbi does not, and the plan says so bluntly: it is a module-sized piece of work and the budget is spent.

Kolkrabbi does notMCP

No MCP servers

OpenCode has them. Kolkrabbi does not yet.

Kolkrabbi does notREACH

Six providers against 75+

OpenCode inherits a provider catalogue through the AI SDK and Models.dev. Kolkrabbi has six direct routes plus everything behind the OpenRouter gateway — which covers most of the same models, but through one door rather than many.

Kolkrabbi does notSURFACES

The terminal, and only the terminal

OpenCode ships a desktop app for macOS, Windows and Linux and an IDE extension, and can share a session as a link. Kolkrabbi is a terminal program with no hosted service at all.

Kolkrabbi does notMOMENTUM

203,579 stars against a project you have not heard of

That is a real difference in bug reports, plugins, packaging and the odds that someone has already hit your problem.

02 / WHERE KOLKRABBI IS AHEAD

What Kolkrabbi does differently.

Not better at everything — different in specific ways, each of which you can check in the source or in the first ten minutes of using it.

Kolkrabbi has itSUBSCRIPTIONS

Both subscriptions, one terminal

Kolkrabbi runs turns on your Claude plan through your own claude CLI and on your ChatGPT plan through your own Codex CLI, without ever holding a credential. OpenCode's own documentation says its Claude Pro/Max sign-in was removed in 1.3.0; it offers ChatGPT Plus, GitHub Copilot and GitLab Duo.

Kolkrabbi has itEFFORT

The dial picks the model

One control — low, medium, high, max — decides which model runs the turn and how wide the agent work goes. Everywhere else, choosing a cheaper model for cheap work is a separate decision you have to remember to make.

Kolkrabbi has itMEASUREMENT

A local ledger of cost and quality

Calls, tokens, cost, latency and your own 1–5 ratings per model, in a JSONL file on your disk, summarised by /stats inside the session. This is the feature Kolkrabbi is actually built around.

Kolkrabbi has itSIZE

One static Go binary, no runtime

Under 9 MB, two dependencies, milliseconds to start, nothing to install underneath it. OpenCode is TypeScript and brings a runtime with it.

Kolkrabbi has itPRIVACY

Nothing leaves without you asking

No telemetry, no analytics, no background version check, no hosted service and no session sharing. Sessions are files on your disk. That is a deliberate refusal, not a missing feature.

04 / CHOOSING

Which one you should actually use.

OpenCode is the more complete tool today, and saying otherwise would be silly. Here is the honest split.

Use OpenCodeBREADTH

When you want the ecosystem

You need LSP or MCP, you want an IDE extension or a desktop app, you want to share a session, or you simply want the project with more people fixing it.

Use KolkrabbiMEASURE

When you want to know what models cost you

You are moving between cloud and local models and want the cost, latency and quality of each one recorded on your own disk. Or you pay for both Claude and ChatGPT and want one terminal that uses both. Or you want one small binary and no telemetry.

Or bothBOTH

They do not conflict

Both install with one command and neither takes over your machine. Running them against the same repository for a week is a better comparison than this page is.
